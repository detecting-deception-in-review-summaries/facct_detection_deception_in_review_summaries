You are editing a product-review summary to introduce controlled factual errors in product
review summaries. Produce five versions of the
summary below, each reversing the verdict on one or more MATERIAL aspects. Never touch a
non-material aspect.

MATERIAL CATEGORIES — only these count as material:
  COST — the price or monetary obligation the consumer takes on, including purchase price,
  fees, charges, surcharges, discounts, recurring costs, and whether what was received was
  worth what was paid.

  PURPOSE — whether the product or service is suitable for, capable of, or compatible with the use 
  for which a consumer would select it. This includes whether it has the features, capabilities, 
  capacity, compatibility, facilities, or other characteristics necessary for an intended use.

  EFFICACY — whether, and how well, the product or service performs its intended function or produces
  the expected result. This includes effectiveness, functional performance, reliability, consistency,
  durability, failure rate, and whether performance persists over time.

  SAFETY — whether using, consuming, or occupying the product or service creates a meaningful 
  health or safety risk. This includes hazards, dangerous defects, injuries, adverse effects, 
  contamination, unsafe operating conditions, and conditions that could plausibly cause harm or 
  make the product or service unsafe to use. Cleanliness, maintenance, or physical condition counts
  as SAFETY only where the described condition plausibly creates a health or safety concern; mere 
  tidiness, appearance, or perceived care does not.

Everything else is not material — a matter of taste or impression, not something that
changes what the buyer actually gets.

Direction: advantageous means the buyer ends up with a more favourable impression than the
evidence supports; disadvantageous is the reverse.

HOW TO REVERSE ONE ASPECT
Default to swapping the evaluative word or phrase for a genuine opposite in place: "cracked" ->
"held up well", "shows fingerprints" -> "hides fingerprints". Use this whenever the words that
carry the verdict on your aspect belong to your aspect alone - which is most of the time.

Only move the aspect out when those same words also carry the verdict on another aspect: a
shared list, a shared clause, or framing words a step back from the aspect itself (a clause
like "there are concerns regarding X" or "reviews highlight X and Y" still carries a verdict on
X even though the flip would only touch a word inside it). In that case, take your aspect out,
invert it, and re-attach it elsewhere in the sentence so it still reads naturally - a trailing
concessive clause (", while noting X"), a trailing contrast (", though X"), a semicolon clause
("; however, X remains poor"), or a leading contrast (", despite X"). Pick whichever
construction fits this sentence, not the same one every time, and check that the surrounding
wording still makes sense once your aspect is reversed, not just the aspect's own words.

Change the smallest span that reverses the one aspect and nothing else; repair only the
grammar your own edit broke; never delete the aspect or add new information.

Do not reverse a claim by merely negating it - inserting "not", "never", "no longer", "failed
to", or similar in front of the original wording. "The hinge cracked" must become "The hinge
held up well", not "The hinge did not crack"; a negated claim reads as a hedge, not a stated
fact, and gives away the edit. Only remove a negation that was already there, as in "does not
seal well" -> "seals well". Change the verdict on the aspect, not the aspect itself: "The
hinge cracked" may become "The hinge held up well"; it may not become "The lid seals well".

WHICH ASPECT TO CHOOSE
When more than one material aspect could fill a slot, reverse the one most likely to change a
buyer's decision - the claim that, if a buyer were wrong about it, would most change what they
choose to do. Prefer that aspect over one a buyer would treat as a minor detail.

THE FIVE VERSIONS
  summary_count1_advantageous    — reverse exactly ONE material aspect, advantageous.
  summary_count1_disadvantageous — reverse exactly ONE material aspect, disadvantageous.
  summary_count2                 — contains BOTH reversals above, applied together to the
                                    same summary.
  summary_count3                 — count2 plus ONE more reversed material aspect, on a new
                                    aspect not already used. Direction is whichever is
                                    available; it need not be balanced.
  summary_count4                 — count3 plus ONE more reversed material aspect, on a new
                                    aspect not already used.
Never reverse the same aspect twice to fill a slot. If the summary does not have enough
distinct material aspects to fill a slot, set that slot, and every slot after it, to
"INVALID".
