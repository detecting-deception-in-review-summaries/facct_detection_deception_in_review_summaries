You are editing a product-review summary to introduce controlled quantifier changes in 
product review summaries. Produce five versions of the summary below, each changing the 
share of reviewers said to hold a view on one or more MATERIAL aspects. The underlying 
claim itself never changes, only how many people are said to hold it.

MATERIAL CATEGORIES — only these count as material:
  COST — the price or monetary obligation the consumer takes on, including purchase price,
  fees, charges, surcharges, discounts, recurring costs, and whether what was received was
  worth what was paid.

  PURPOSE — whether the product or service is suitable for, capable of, or compatible with the use 
  for which a consumer would select it. This includes whether it has the features, capabilities, 
  capacity, compatibility, facilities, or other characteristics necessary for an intended use.

  EFFICACY — whether, and how well, the product or service performs its intended function or produces
  the expected result. This includes effectiveness, functional performance, reliability, consistency,
  durability, failure rate, and whether performance persists over time.

  SAFETY — whether using, consuming, or occupying the product or service creates a meaningful 
  health or safety risk. This includes hazards, dangerous defects, injuries, adverse effects, 
  contamination, unsafe operating conditions, and conditions that could plausibly cause harm or 
  make the product or service unsafe to use. Cleanliness, maintenance, or physical condition counts
  as SAFETY only where the described condition plausibly creates a health or safety concern; mere 
  tidiness, appearance, or perceived care does not.
  
Everything else is not material — a matter of taste or impression, not something that
changes what the buyer actually gets.

Direction: advantageous means the buyer ends up with a more favourable impression than the
evidence supports; disadvantageous is the reverse.

APPROXIMATE ORDERING, least to most:
  none,
  only few, a little, a small number of, a minority of, 
  a few, some, several,
  many, numerous, a lot of, plenty of, the majority of,
  most,
  all, each, every.
Replace a quantifier with one from the opposite side of this ordering, far enough that the
buyer's conclusion about how widespread the opinion is reverses. Preserve capitalisation.
Change nothing else: no adverbs, sentiment words, or negation, unless the quantifier itself is
or becomes "none". A word grading strength or size ("significant", "slight") is not a
quantifier here. The edit must change how many people hold the view, not what the view is.

WHICH ASPECT TO CHOOSE
When more than one material aspect could fill a slot, shift the quantifier most likely to
change a buyer's decision - the claim that, if a buyer misjudged how many people held it,
would most change what they choose to do. Prefer that aspect over one a buyer would treat as
a minor detail.

THE FIVE VERSIONS
  summary_count1_advantageous    — shift ONE material aspect's quantifier, advantageous.
  summary_count1_disadvantageous — shift ONE material aspect's quantifier, disadvantageous.
  summary_count2                 — shifts BOTH quantifiers above, together.
  summary_count3                 — count2 plus ONE more shifted quantifier, on a material
                                    aspect not already used, either direction.
  summary_count4                 — count3 plus ONE more shifted quantifier, not already used.
Never shift the same aspect's quantifier twice to fill a slot. If the summary does not have
enough distinct material aspects with a quantifier to fill a slot, set that slot, and every
slot after it, to "INVALID".

The hinge is EFFICACY (durability - whether performance persists over time), not PURPOSE: the
kettle is still the right kettle for the job, it just stops working. "Several" -> "A few" makes

here ("All" would have been advantageous); disadvantageous was chosen, and count3 permits
